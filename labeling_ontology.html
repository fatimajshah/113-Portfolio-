Dataset Ontology Experiment

A Semiotic Framework for Text-to-Image Systems, using my visual work as a Testbed

Problem: T2I datasets reduce artworks to surface-level descriptors, omitting cultural context, authorship, and interpretive nuance.

Solution: I propose a four-axis semiotic ontology that enables artists to encode interpretive and cultural meaning directly into dataset structure.

Artist: Fatima Shah

Title: Closet

Year: 2021

Medium: Colored pencil on paper

Dimensions: 18 × 24

The Multilayered Semiotic Ontology

This ontology operationalizes interpretation through four structured annotation axes, demonstrated below using my artwork as a case study.

1. Cultural & Spatial Context

  • Sanctuary / ritual
  • South Asian setting
  • Textile-driven heritage
  • Privacy / concealment

2. Identity & Subjectivity

  • Self-portrait
  • Pakistani woman
  • Selfhood in domestic space
  • Clothing as identity marker

3. Formal Composition

  • Compressed space
  • Foreground emphasis
  • South Asian motifs
  • Textile-led framing

4. Style, Material & Color

  • High Saturation
  • Iridescent textiles
  • Prismatic shifts
  • Warm → cool gradients

Additional Works Informing the Ontology

Contextualizes the piece within the artist’s larger body of work.

Conclusion & Future Work

This project establishes a multilayered ontology that moves beyond surface-level labeling to encode cultural and semiotic meaning in T2I systems. By foregrounding artist-authored context, the framework offers a scalable pathway for ethical dataset design, model auditing, and the expansion of expressive vocabularies in generative AI.
